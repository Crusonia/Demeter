November 21, 2026. This appraisal feeds [progression and reversal (#57)](https://github.com/Crusonia/Demeter/issues/57), [dietary pathway evaluation (#58)](https://github.com/Crusonia/Demeter/issues/58), and the [community data request (#115)](https://github.com/Crusonia/Demeter/issues/115). The design trace is I-01/I-05/I-07/I-11/I-12 → F-04/F-08 → T-06/T-08. The [clinical observation contract](CLINICAL_OBSERVATION_CONTRACT.md) defines how source observations map to the model. The counts below come from public publications and serve as source checks. No participant records were used in this appraisal.

## CORDIOPREV: two assigned diets and annual diabetes assessments

CORDIOPREV randomized people with established coronary heart disease in Spain to either a Mediterranean diet rich in olive oil or a low-fat diet rich in complex carbohydrates, alongside standard cardiac care. The public registry snapshot, last updated June 3, 2021, lists incident T2D as a secondary outcome and contains no posted results. The parent trial primarily evaluated cardiovascular events. The T2D substudies describe annual assessments using fasting plasma glucose, a two-hour oral glucose tolerance test, and HbA1c. The 2024 report describes repeat-test confirmation when hyperglycemia is equivocal. The public counts do not give the time from the last negative to the first qualifying or confirming test for each case. The dietary assignment is a comparison of two supported regimens. Study targets, reported intake and an individual consumed dose have different roles. Even a complete arm-specific incidence analysis would first estimate the effect of *assignment* on a recorded endpoint in this coronary population. A nutrient or UPF dose-response relationship, a biological lag, separate progression/recovery effects, and U.S. transport each require additional evidence. CORDIOPREV enrolled separately from [PREDIMED-Reus](REUS_DIABETES_PATHWAY.md). Its comparator, counseling context, population and ascertainment must be matched before using it for external prediction. Its derivative publications reuse the same trial and the already inspected outcomes cannot serve as an untouched holdout. [Dietary intake measurements](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-022-02854-7)

The smallest useful public addition is a disclosure-reviewed table by assigned arm, baseline phenotype and follow-up window. Each row should state the at-risk denominator and distinguish first confirmed diagnoses, deaths before diagnosis, last contact or permanent withdrawal, and pending or missed tests with possible return. Specify how deaths after diagnosis are recorded, identify source definitions and rounding/suppression, and reconcile each cohort total. That would support appraisal of time-specific *first recorded diagnosis* under competing events. Paired glycemic labels with prior diagnosis and medication history would be needed for the progression/reversal analysis. The trial authors describe institutional collaboration under an accepted proposal and agreement. The public articles and registry are the available basis for this appraisal. [Trial data availability](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-025-02942-y)

## MIDUS: define the biomarker follow-up denominator

The MIDUS 3 Biomarker Project summary defines its eligible frame using survey participation, prior biomarker completion or twin recruitment, and ability to travel safely. Its overall response calculation includes eligible people who could not be reached, while its adjusted calculation excludes them. This frame also includes newly recruited twins who did not participate in the baseline biomarker project. The separate UW–Madison project flow chart lists a different response percentage for MIDUS 3 Biomarker without a denominator in the chart. Its sample guide and cross-project tables describe survey retention and same-wave project participation. The published rates have different or unresolved denominators and should not be applied as retention probabilities for the baseline biomarker cohort. [Project summary, pp. 1–2](https://github.com/midus-study/public-documentation/blob/e1c99fcda5c3bb676d847787c38f53d02b88e5d8/M3P4/Documentation/M3_P4_Biomarker_Project_Summary.pdf) · [Project flow chart, p. 7](https://midus.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Understanding_MIDUS_Data_Collection_20250306.pdf) · [Sample guide, Table 7](https://midus.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Understanding_MIDUS_Samples_20250312.pdf) · [Cross-project participation](https://midus.wisc.edu/cross_proj/index.php)

The core mortality memo documents submission to the National Death Index conditional on sufficient matching information. Its completeness statements refer to final versus early-release search periods. The decedent release and that memo do not enumerate death-linkage eligibility, verified survival or last contact for each baseline biomarker member. The released death month/year identifies a window. A missing match leaves the vital status unresolved unless another source confirms it. [Core mortality memo, p. 1](https://github.com/midus-study/public-documentation/blob/e1c99fcda5c3bb676d847787c38f53d02b88e5d8/Mortality/Core/MIDUS_Core_DocumentationOfMortality_20260511.pdf)

Li et al. (2026) use paired MIDUS waves to predict movements between clusters of modeled diabetes risk. Their article states that paired records were required and that imputation can leave differential attrition bias. Its cluster movement and hypothetical feature perturbations address prediction over a wave interval. They cannot resolve biomarker invitation and death ascertainment for the full baseline frame, link annual glycemic states, or estimate a causal diet effect. [Published MIDUS prediction study](https://doi.org/10.2196/87374)

The smallest useful addition is a steward-approved MIDUS 2 Biomarker inception-frame coverage table, including the national and Milwaukee samples. It must reconcile invitation, eligibility (including health/travel restrictions), noncontact, refusal, visit completion, missed specimens, and last contact, together with death-linkage eligibility, match status and observation closeout. Later-wave entrants need their own denominator. If the coverage table is available, review assay comparability and medication history before proposing a likelihood. The [longitudinal source requirements](PUBLIC_LONGITUDINAL_DATA_REQUIREMENTS.md) describe the field mapping and proposed scientific review.
