# Externalities and the business mechanisms they might support

Status: **candidate premises for appraisal**, not measured impacts or business
findings. These entries feed the [input inventory](04_MODEL_INPUTS.md) and
[formulation contracts](05_FORMULATION_AND_TESTS.md). They are part of the design
foundation for identifying opportunities and costs across the value chain.

## Keep the accounts distinct

An externality is a consequence for others that the originating decision does
not fully account for through prices or compensation. Its classification depends
on who decides, who is affected, and the institutional boundary. The IMF's
[explanation of externalities](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/externalities)
provides this economic distinction; it does not establish the Demeter-specific
pathways below.

Separate **private value**, **payments/transfers**, and **uncompensated spillovers**.
Retail margins, a producer premium, household health gains, and avoided insured
claims are not all externalities. Pooled/public healthcare effects require an
explicit coverage and financing boundary before classifying any external portion.
Benefits to a household's own health remain important even when they are private.

Positive/negative below describes a recipient's benefit/cost relative to a stated
counterfactual. It is unrelated to reinforcing/balancing loop polarity. A change
can help one group and harm another. Do not count avoiding a negative externality
again as an independent positive benefit; several entries are alternative views
of the same underlying physical pathway.

## Benefits and costs that belong in different accounts

| Effect | Default treatment in the design |
| --- | --- |
| A buyer voluntarily pays a product premium | A price and transfer; estimate quantity/cost changes and who retains margin separately. The premium is not itself an external benefit. |
| A farmer's yield or input-cost change on the same farm | Private production effect; any spillover to other sites requires a separate pathway. |
| A person's own health gain | Private health outcome; any caregiver or pooled-resource spillover is separately identified. |
| A payer saves claims within its own contract | Actor budget outcome; classify a residual external portion only after specifying coverage and financing. |
| A food company receives a payer outcome payment | Revenue to the company and expense to the payer; not another copy of the health/resource benefit. |
| Incumbent revenue falls as purchases shift | Competitive/pecuniary redistribution by default; count real stranded resources or uncompensated harm only with a distinct mechanism. |
| A credit or subsidy pays for an environmental service | A payment/internalization mechanism; retain the underlying physical effect and any residual spillover without counting the payment as extra social benefit. |

## From externality to a business hypothesis

For each opportunity, specify the changed physical outcome, responsible action,
beneficiary, potential paying customer, and provider capable of delivering it.
Record a measurable counterfactual, eligibility, attribution, verification costs,
contract timing, retention, performance risk, and the portion left uncompensated.
Keep regulatory or contractual arrangements as explicit scenario terms; no legal
entitlement or available subsidy is assumed by this register.

Compare **no payment**, **a specified payment arrangement**, and **failure to verify
or deliver**. A large social benefit with no reachable payer is not automatically
revenue. Report private operating value, the actual payment, and residual
spillovers separately, with uncertainty. Keep physical units when monetization
cannot be supported; do not invent a shadow price to produce a dollar total.

Each future entry needs its baseline, action, causal links, recipient boundary,
units, evidence keys, uncertainty, overlapping benefits, proposed payment rule,
and disconfirming observation. Revise its status when appraised; preserve rejected
business hypotheses as part of the design record.
