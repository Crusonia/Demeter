# Stakeholder rigidities

Across agriculture, food, and health, experienced people hold firm beliefs that a better, cheaper system is not possible: "healthy food can't be affordable," "people are addicted to junk food," "prevention savings go to the next insurer." Some are real constraints. Many are true only under today's prices, rules, and time horizons. Demeter treats each as a hypothesis to test.

The full register, with stable IDs (B-01 to B-49) and links to the mechanisms that would test each belief, is in the [design registers](https://github.com/Crusonia/Demeter/blob/main/docs/design/06_STAKEHOLDER_RIGIDITIES.md).

## Four kinds of rigidity

| Type | Source | How it moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biological | Biology or physics | Rarely; design around it. |
| Economic | Prices, costs, scale | When cost curves or demand shift. |
| Institutional | Rules, contracts, subsidies, budget scoring, payment models | When the institution changes. |
| Belief | Narrative, habit, professional norms | With evidence and experience. |

## What a belief claims versus what the evidence shows

| The belief claims | Claim does not hold | Claim holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Change is impossible** | **Misperceived constraints**, the main opportunity. Examples: reformulation kills sales; patients won't change their diet; health and climate are separate problems. Some are already eroding: "food isn't a medical benefit" is weakening as Medicaid and Medicare Advantage add food benefits. | **Structural locks** that need new contracts, payment models, or rules rather than persuasion. Examples: insurer member turnover; fee-for-service payment; ten-year budget scoring. |
| **A fix or outcome is easy or assured** | **Traps** where optimism runs ahead of evidence. Examples: soil carbon credits as reliable revenue; access alone fixing diets; prevention always saving money; drugs making food irrelevant. | Accurate optimism, which is not a rigidity. |

## Two patterns behind many locks

1. **Who pays is not who benefits.** The farmer, grocer, or household paying for a better diet is rarely the insurer, employer, or public budget that captures the gain.
2. **Short horizons meet long delays.** Insurer tenure, budget windows, fund lifecycles, and election cycles are shorter than the years or decades biology and agriculture take to respond.

Neither pattern says a better system is impossible. Both are about who captures the gain, and when.

## How the model uses the register

Every rigidity is registered, but only a few are modeled at a time. Sensitivity analysis decides which ones materially change the [top-level metrics](Top-Level-Metrics.md). Resolving a rigidity means finding out whether it is real, when it breaks, what breaking it costs, and who gains. It does not mean assuming it is false.
